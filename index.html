About

About Me

I am an education researcher from Bangladesh interested in understanding how people learn, think, and develop independent judgment in an age of intelligent technologies. My research is guided by a central question: "How can education cultivate independent thinking while responsibly integrating artificial intelligence into teaching and learning?"

My current research focuses on generative AI in higher education. I investigate how AI shapes learning, metacognition, and assessment, with particular attention to when it strengthens students' thinking and when it begins to replace the cognitive processes required for independent competence.

I currently serve as Research & Grants Lead at Altlearn, where I lead education research and evidence-informed programme development across social-emotional learning, AI-supported pedagogy, career education, and STEM initiatives. My work spans teacher professional development, AI literacy, mixed-methods evaluation, grant development, and monitoring, evaluation, and learning (MEAL).

Previously, as a Leadership Fellow at Teach For Bangladesh, I designed more than 332 technology-enhanced lessons and contributed to initiatives that reached over 1,800 students and 3,000 community members. That experience grounded my research questions in real classroom constraints rather than abstract theory.

My path into education research was shaped not by a laboratory but by the classroom. Although I began my academic journey in accounting, my experience as an educator transformed my interests and led me to investigate how people learn, think, and develop the capacity for independent problem-solving. That experience continues to guide my work as I explore how artificial intelligence can be designed to support, rather than replace, human cognition and learning.

Research Focus

Research Project · Aug 2026 – Dec 2026

AI Scaffolding vs. AI Substitution

This project examines the conditions under which generative AI functions as a cognitive scaffold that supports learning versus the conditions under which it replaces independent thinking. Drawing on theories of cognitive offloading, metacognition, and learning transfer, it investigates how different levels of AI support influence cognitive autonomy, self-regulation, and long-term knowledge retention.

MEd Thesis · Jan 2026 – Oct 2026

AI Readiness in Bangladeshi Schools

This study explores the readiness of government secondary schools in Dhaka, Bangladesh, to integrate artificial intelligence into teaching and learning practices. Focusing on head teachers, teachers, and students, the project examines institutional preparedness, perceived opportunities and challenges, and the factors that may support effective and equitable AI implementation.

Multi-Year Research Initiative · 2022 – 2026 · Co-Investigator

ProjectGPT

A multi-year research initiative investigating the psychological, pedagogical, and institutional implications of generative AI in education. This initiative has produced three peer-reviewed publications to date: a study on the effects of ChatGPT on creative writing (Journal of Computer Assisted Learning, 2024), an investigation into the factors influencing AI chatbot usage among students (Computers and Education: AI, 2024), and an examination of AI chatbots as classroom pedagogical tools (Computers and Education: AI, 2025).

Role: Co-Investigator. Contributed to research design, systematic literature reviews, data collection and quantitative analysis, and manuscript preparation across all three studies.
